# Your first conversation with Claude

A conversation with Claude is a thread: messages from you, replies from Claude, back and forth. The thread *remembers* itself — Claude can see everything you've said earlier in the same conversation. The thread does **not** remember itself across separate chats. Open a new chat and you're starting clean.

That single rule shapes how to use it well.

## A first prompt that almost always works

Three pieces, in this order:

1. **What you want** — say it directly. *"Draft a polite email to my landlord asking to fix the AC."*
2. **Who it's for** — *"My landlord is friendly but not great with email."*
3. **How long / what shape** — *"Under 100 words. One paragraph. End with a clear ask."*

That's it. Most failed prompts are just missing one of those three.

## When it doesn't land the first time

You don't fix a prompt by getting cleverer — you fix it by being more specific about what didn't work. Three moves that fix most disappointments:

## When to start a fresh chat

A new conversation is a *clean room*. Use a fresh chat when:

- You're switching tasks entirely (drafting an email → debugging a spreadsheet)
- The current thread is long and Claude is getting distracted by earlier context
- You want to share the conversation with someone and don't want them seeing the earlier stuff
- You think Claude has "locked in" on a wrong assumption and you can't shake it loose

A long thread isn't always bad — *related* follow-ups are exactly what threads are for. Switching topics inside one thread is what causes most "Claude got worse" feelings.
