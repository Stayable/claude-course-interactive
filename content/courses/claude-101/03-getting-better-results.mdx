# Getting better results

Lesson 02 covered the three pieces of a working first prompt. This one is the next layer: four patterns that consistently raise the quality of whatever you get back. None of them are clever. They're just things most people leave out.

## 1. Give Claude the material

Most of what people want from Claude is *about something specific* — a document, an email thread, a transcript, a snippet of code. Pasting the actual content in beats describing it every time.

<Compare
  leftLabel="Describing the material"
  rightLabel="Pasting the material"
  left={[
    "'Summarize that long article about interest rates.'",
    "'My boss wrote me a long email — give me a one-line response.'",
    "'There's a bug in my function that splits strings.'"
  ]}
  right={[
    "'Summarize this article in 3 bullets:\\n\\n[paste]'",
    "'Reply to this email in one sentence, agreeing but pushing the meeting to Friday:\\n\\n[paste]'",
    "'This function should split on commas but is splitting on every character. What's wrong?\\n\\n[paste code]'"
  ]}
  takeaway="Don't make Claude guess what you're talking about. Paste it."
/>

## 2. Show an example of what good looks like

If you've got even one example of the output you want, give it. Claude will match the *shape* of the example almost every time — length, tone, structure, level of detail.

<TypingDemo
  prompt={`Rewrite my product update in the style of the example below.

Example (the style I want):
"Shipped: faster image search. Up to 5x quicker on libraries over 10k images. Available now in 1.4.0."

My update (rewrite to match the example):
"We have released the new version of our application which includes various improvements to the image searching functionality, making it considerably faster especially on larger libraries."`}
  response={`Shipped: faster image search. Noticeably quicker on large libraries. Available now in 1.4.0.`}
/>

One example is a hint. Three examples is a pattern Claude will follow tightly.

## 3. Tell Claude what role it's playing

A short role line at the top of a prompt shifts the whole answer. Claude isn't *pretending* — it's matching the vocabulary, depth, and care that role would bring.

<Diagram
  takeaway="The same question, given a role, gets a different and usually better answer."
  chart={`flowchart LR
    Q["'How should I price this?'"]
    Q --> A["No role<br/>Generic 4-step framework"]
    Q --> B["'You're a SaaS CFO.'<br/>Margin, CAC payback, anchor pricing"]
    Q --> C["'You're a pricing coach for solopreneurs.'<br/>Skip the corporate stuff, get to a number"]`}
/>

Some roles that punch above their weight: *experienced editor*, *patient tutor*, *skeptical reviewer*, *terse senior engineer*, *plain-language translator*.

## 4. Ask for the format

You'll re-read or re-use the output more easily if it lands in the shape you need. Ask for it.

- "Reply in a table with columns: name, role, decision."
- "Bullets, 5–7 words each."
- "One short paragraph, no bullets, no headers."
- "Return only the rewritten sentence. No commentary."

That last one is a quiet superpower. "Return only X" trims the *"Sure! Here's…"* preamble Claude often adds.
