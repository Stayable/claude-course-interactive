You talk to it like you'd talk to a thoughtful colleague — in plain language — and it answers back in plain language. The thing doing the answering is a *model*: a very large neural network trained on text. You don't need to know how the network works to use Claude well. You just need to know what to ask, and how to read what comes back.

<Callout kind="tip">
If you've used a chatbot before, you already know the basic shape: you type, it replies. The difference with Claude is how far you can push that conversation — long documents, real code, multi-step thinking — without it falling over.
</Callout>

## Where Claude lives

Claude shows up in a few places. Each one is the same model underneath, but the surface is shaped for a different kind of work.

<SurfaceMap takeaway="Same Claude, different shells. Pick the surface that matches the work." />

You can start anywhere. Most people start in **Chat** (claude.ai in a browser), graduate to the **Desktop app** when they're using it daily, and step into **Cowork** or **Code** when they want Claude editing real files.

## What a reply looks like

Claude doesn't answer all at once — it *streams*, one word at a time, so you can read along as it thinks. That little blinking cursor below is showing the same thing you'll see for real on claude.ai.

<TypingDemo
  prompt="Explain what a large language model is, in one paragraph, to a curious 10-year-old."
  response={`A large language model is like a very, very well-read friend. It has read more books and websites than any person could in a lifetime, and it has gotten good at one specific game: guessing what word comes next.

When you ask it a question, it doesn't look the answer up in a database. It writes one word, then guesses the next, then the next — and the answers come out shaped like the things it has seen humans write. That's why it sounds natural. It's also why it can sometimes sound confident about things that aren't quite right.`}
/>

## What it's good at — and where to be careful

Claude is strong at language tasks: anything that's mostly *reading*, *writing*, or *thinking out loud about text you've given it*. It's less reliable when the task requires fresh information from the outside world, or when small errors carry big consequences.

<Compare
  leftLabel="Claude is good at"
  rightLabel="Be careful when…"
  left={[
    "Drafting emails, posts, reports",
    "Summarizing long documents you paste in",
    "Answering questions about content you give it",
    "Writing and reviewing code",
    "Walking through reasoning step by step",
    "Translating, rephrasing, simplifying"
  ]}
  right={[
    "You need today's news, prices, or scores — it doesn't browse by default",
    "An exact citation matters — verify the source yourself",
    "The task is high-stakes (legal, medical, financial) without a human checking",
    "You want it to remember last week's chat — each new conversation starts fresh",
    "It sounds confident — confidence isn't proof"
  ]}
  takeaway="Use Claude where the work is text-shaped. Bring outside facts and final judgment yourself."
/>
