# Connecting your tools

Out of the box, Claude can read what you paste and write what you ask for. But it can't look in your email, your drive, your Slack, or your CRM unless you give it a way in. **Connectors** are those ways in.

A connector hooks Claude up to one outside app — securely, on your terms. You stay in control of *what* it can see and *when*.

## What a connector unlocks

Once a tool is connected, the conversation can do things like:

- *"Pull up the deck I made with Priya last month and update slide 4."*
- *"Summarize this week's #launch-prep channel — what's blocking the release?"*
- *"What's on my calendar tomorrow afternoon? Find me a 30-min slot to call my mom."*
- *"In our CRM, list every account in the financial-services segment with no activity in 60 days."*

The pattern is always the same: you ask in plain language, Claude figures out which connector(s) it needs, asks for the relevant data, and answers.

## What's underneath

The protocol Claude uses to talk to tools is called **MCP** — the Model Context Protocol. You don't need to know how it works to use a connector, but it's worth knowing the name. MCP is open: developers can build their own connectors for tools that don't have one yet.

If you ever want to go deeper — building a connector, exposing your own internal tool to Claude — there's a dedicated **Introduction to Model Context Protocol** course. For Claude 101, the takeaway is just: *Claude can reach out to your apps, but only the ones you've handed a key to.*

## A small mental model

A useful way to think about connectors:

- **Without connectors** — Claude is a smart colleague locked in a room. You bring it material; it works on whatever you bring.
- **With connectors** — That colleague can step out of the room to look something up, but only into the rooms you've unlocked.
