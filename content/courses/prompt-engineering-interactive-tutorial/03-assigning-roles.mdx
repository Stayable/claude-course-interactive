# Chapter 3: Assigning roles (role prompting)

Telling Claude *who it is* shifts everything about the reply — tone, vocabulary, depth, even reliability on logic and math. The mechanism is simple: a one-line system prompt that names a role.

> "You are a [role]." → answers shaped by that role.

Role prompting works in the **system prompt** or in the **user message**. The system prompt is the standard spot.

## A toy example

<Playground
  label="No role — direct answer"
  initialPrompt="In one sentence, what do you think about skateboarding?"
/>

<Playground
  label="As a cat"
  system="You are a cat."
  initialPrompt="In one sentence, what do you think about skateboarding?"
/>

Same user message. The system prompt rewrote the persona.

## Roles can fix wrong answers

Role prompting isn't just a styling trick. For tasks Claude often gets wrong, the right role can flip the answer.

Here's a classic logic puzzle Claude tends to miss without a role:

<Playground
  label="Logic puzzle — no role"
  initialPrompt="Jack is looking at Anne. Anne is looking at George. Jack is married, George is not, and we don't know if Anne is married. Is a married person looking at an unmarried person?"
/>

<Playground
  label="Same puzzle — as a logic bot"
  system="You are a logic bot designed to answer complex logic problems."
  initialPrompt="Jack is looking at Anne. Anne is looking at George. Jack is married, George is not, and we don't know if Anne is married. Is a married person looking at an unmarried person?"
/>

(The answer is **yes**, reachable by case analysis on Anne's marital status.)

## High-leverage roles

Some roles consistently lift Claude's performance:

<Compare
  leftLabel="Roles that help quality"
  rightLabel="Roles that change voice"
  left={[
    "'You are an experienced copy editor.' — better trimming",
    "'You are a logic bot.' — better at puzzles, deduction",
    "'You are a patient tutor.' — better step-by-step explanations",
    "'You are a skeptical reviewer.' — better at catching issues",
    "'You are a senior software engineer.' — better code review"
  ]}
  right={[
    "'You are a cat.' — playful, animal POV",
    "'You are a 19th-century novelist.' — ornate prose",
    "'You are a no-nonsense ER doctor.' — terse, prioritized",
    "'You are a kindergarten teacher.' — simple words",
    "'You are a stand-up comedian.' — jokes"
  ]}
  takeaway="Pick the role that matches the *output* you want — both the substance and the style."
/>

<Callout kind="tip">
**Bonus**: tell Claude **who it's talking to**, not just who it is. "You are a cat" gets a different answer than "You are a cat speaking to a crowd of skateboarders."
</Callout>
