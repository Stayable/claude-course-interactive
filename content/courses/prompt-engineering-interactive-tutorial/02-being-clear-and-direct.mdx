# Chapter 2: Being clear and direct

Claude responds best to **clear and direct** instructions. Think of Claude like a smart new colleague who's never done the task before. The more concretely you explain what you want, the better the answer.

**The Golden Rule of Clear Prompting:**

> Show your prompt to a colleague and have them try to follow it. If they're confused, Claude will be too.

## Same task, two prompts

<Compare
  leftLabel="❌ Vague"
  rightLabel="✅ Clear"
  left={[
    "'Write a haiku about robots.' (often gets 'Here is a haiku:' preamble)",
    "'Who's the best basketball player?' (Claude hedges, lists 3-4 names)",
    "'Improve this email.' (vague; what kind of improvement?)"
  ]}
  right={[
    "'Write a haiku about robots. Skip the preamble; go straight into the poem.'",
    "'Who's the best basketball player? If you absolutely had to pick one, who would it be?'",
    "'Tighten this email by 30%. Keep the ask in the last line. Drop the apologies.'"
  ]}
  takeaway="Being clear is mostly being specific about what you don't want."
/>

## Forcing a decision

When you want Claude to commit instead of hedging, *tell it to commit*.
