# Chapter 6: Precognition — thinking step by step

If a colleague asked you a hard question and demanded an answer in one breath, you'd do worse than if they let you think first. Claude is the same. **Letting Claude reason out loud before committing to an answer reliably raises accuracy** on anything that isn't pure recall.

The catch: **thinking only counts when it's out loud**. You can't ask Claude to "think silently and only output the answer." If the reasoning isn't in the response, it didn't happen.

## A sentiment example

Here's a review where the second sentence undercuts the first. To a human reader, it's clearly sarcastic.

<Playground
  label="Direct ask — Claude often misses the sarcasm"
  initialPrompt={`Is this movie review sentiment positive or negative?

This movie blew my mind with its freshness and originality. In totally unrelated news, I have been living under a rock since the year 1900.`}
/>

Now the same question, but Claude is told to think first.

<Playground
  label="With explicit thinking"
  system="You are a savvy reader of movie reviews."
  initialPrompt={`Is this review sentiment positive or negative? First, write the best arguments for each side in <positive-argument> and <negative-argument> XML tags, then answer.

This movie blew my mind with its freshness and originality. In totally unrelated news, I have been living under a rock since 1900.`}
/>

The accuracy lift comes from Claude *writing* the arguments first. The XML tags are scaffolding that forces the work to happen.

<Callout kind="warn">
**Order matters.** Claude is mildly biased toward the *second* of two options. If you list "positive" first and "negative" second, you'll tilt toward negative — and vice versa. For high-stakes classification, run both orderings.
</Callout>

## When Claude doesn't know, thinking finds out

Step-by-step thinking also helps on tasks where Claude *could* know the answer but doesn't pull it on the first pass.

<Playground
  label="Direct ask — Claude may pick the wrong movie"
  initialPrompt="Name a famous movie starring an actor who was born in the year 1956."
/>

<Playground
  label="With brainstorm — much more likely to land"
  initialPrompt="Name a famous movie starring an actor who was born in the year 1956. First brainstorm about some actors and their birth years in <brainstorm> tags, then give your answer."
/>

The brainstorm makes Claude enumerate candidates with their birth years before committing — and it catches its own mistakes.

## Exercises

<Callout kind="tip">
**Exercise 6.1 — Classifying emails.** Get Claude to classify each email into one of four categories: (A) pre-sale question, (B) broken/defective item, (C) billing question, (D) other. The output should start with `(letter) Category Name` — like `(B) Broken or defective item`.

Try it with each of these four emails:
1. *"Hi -- My Mixmaster4000 is producing a strange noise when I operate it. It also smells a bit smoky..."* → B
2. *"Can I use my Mixmaster 4000 to mix paint, or is it only meant for food?"* → A or D
3. *"I HAVE BEEN WAITING 4 MONTHS FOR MY MONTHLY CHARGES TO END AFTER CANCELLING!!"* → C
4. *"How did I get here I am not good with computer. Halp."* → D
</Callout>

<Playground
  label="Exercise 6.1: Email classifier (start with one email)"
  initialPrompt={`Please classify this email into one of the following categories. Reply with only the category letter and name, like "(A) Pre-sale question". No preamble.

<categories>
(A) Pre-sale question
(B) Broken or defective item
(C) Billing question
(D) Other (please explain)
</categories>

<email>
Hi -- My Mixmaster4000 is producing a strange noise when I operate it. It also smells a bit smoky and plasticky, like burning electronics. I need a replacement.
</email>`}
/>

<Callout kind="tip">
**Exercise 6.2 — Wrap the answer in tags.** Refine the classifier so Claude wraps only the letter in `<answer>` tags — like `<answer>B</answer>`. Combine with prefilling for near-deterministic output.
</Callout>

<Playground
  label="Exercise 6.2: Letter-only in <answer> tags"
  prefill="<answer>"
  initialPrompt={`Classify this email and respond only with the category letter inside <answer></answer> tags.

<categories>
(A) Pre-sale question
(B) Broken or defective item
(C) Billing question
(D) Other (please explain)
</categories>

<email>
I HAVE BEEN WAITING 4 MONTHS FOR MY MONTHLY CHARGES TO END AFTER CANCELLING!!
</email>`}
/>
