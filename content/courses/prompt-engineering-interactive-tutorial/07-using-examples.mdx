# Chapter 7: Using examples (few-shot prompting)

The single most reliable way to control Claude's tone, structure, and format is to **show it an example of what you want**. This is called **few-shot prompting** — "n-shot" referring to how many examples are in the prompt. Zero-shot is no examples; one-shot is one; few-shot is two or more.

Examples beat adjectives every time. "Make it casual" is vague. "Make it like this: [example]" is unambiguous.

## Tone via example

The default reply to a kid's question is often too formal. You could describe the tone you want in three paragraphs — or you could show one example.

<Playground
  label="Without an example — formal and robotic"
  initialPrompt="Will Santa bring me presents on Christmas?"
/>

<Playground
  label="With one example — warm, parent-like tone"
  initialPrompt={`Please complete the conversation by writing the next line, speaking as "A".
Q: Is the tooth fairy real?
A: Of course, sweetie. Wrap up your tooth and put it under your pillow tonight. There might be something waiting for you in the morning.
Q: Will Santa bring me presents on Christmas?`}
/>

Same Claude, vastly different voice. The example does the work.

## Format via example

Few-shot is especially powerful for **structured extraction**. Instead of describing the format, show it.

<Playground
  label="Few-shot extraction — Claude follows the pattern"
  prefill="<individuals>"
  initialPrompt={`Silvermist Hollow, a charming village, was home to an extraordinary group of individuals.
Among them was Dr. Liam Patel, a neurosurgeon who revolutionized surgical techniques at the regional medical center.
Olivia Chen was an innovative architect who transformed the village's landscape with her sustainable and breathtaking designs.
The local theater was graced by the enchanting symphonies of Ethan Kovacs, a professionally-trained musician and composer.
Isabella Torres, a self-taught chef with a passion for locally sourced ingredients, created a culinary sensation with her farm-to-table restaurant.
<individuals>
1. Dr. Liam Patel [NEUROSURGEON]
2. Olivia Chen [ARCHITECT]
3. Ethan Kovacs [MUSICIAN AND COMPOSER]
4. Isabella Torres [CHEF]
</individuals>

Oak Valley, a charming small town, is home to a remarkable trio of individuals whose skills and dedication have left a lasting impact on the community.
At the town's bustling farmer's market, you'll find Laura Simmons, a passionate organic farmer known for her delicious and sustainably grown produce.
In Oak Valley's community center, Kevin Alvarez, a skilled dance instructor, has brought the joy of movement to people of all ages.
Lastly, Rachel O'Connor, a tireless volunteer, dedicates her time to various charitable initiatives.`}
/>

We never explained the format. Claude inferred it from the one labeled example and applied it to the unlabeled paragraph.

## How many examples?

<Compare
  leftLabel="One example is a hint"
  rightLabel="Three+ examples is a pattern"
  left={[
    "Claude will follow it loosely",
    "Good for capturing voice or rough format",
    "Cheaper — fewer input tokens",
    "Use when the task is forgiving"
  ]}
  right={[
    "Claude will match it tightly",
    "Good for strict formats — JSON shapes, tag schemas, classification labels",
    "More tokens, more reliable",
    "Use when downstream code depends on exact format"
  ]}
  takeaway="More examples = tighter conformance. Pick the minimum that gives you the reliability you need."
/>

<Callout kind="tip">
**Pick examples that cover the tricky cases.** If your classifier has 5 categories and 2 of them are easily confused, your examples should include the *confusable* pair — Claude needs to see how to distinguish them, not how to handle the obvious ones.
</Callout>
